# Shoonaya — Find Your Infinite

**Date:** 2026-10-04
**Session context:** Finalizing Shoonaya's public brand voice before aligning the About page, website, and future social copy
**Category:** decision

## What we decided

Use this as Shoonaya's approved public brand expression:

> **Shoonaya**
>
> *Find your infinite.*
>
> **A daily spiritual sanctuary for sacred time, practice, and connection.**

Describe the product around a real daily need: people with full lives may miss sacred dates or lose touch with their practice. Shoonaya helps them notice local Panchang context, understand observances, and make room for practice and connection.

## Why

“Find your infinite” is the founder's preferred distinctive brand line. “A daily spiritual sanctuary…” gives it a concrete product meaning without making the voice feel preachy. The product explanation should make Panchang, Ekadashi and Amavasya, Japa, daily practice, and scripture clear; Rashiphal, Kundali, Live Darshan, Kul, Mandali, and Tirtha support that central promise.

Shoonaya is rooted in Sanatan Dharma. Where the product includes Sikh, Jain, and Buddhist experiences, public copy must name those paths distinctly and avoid presenting them as one blended doctrine.

## Constraints this creates

- Use the approved name, tagline, and descriptor consistently across public pages, metadata, share cards, onboarding, and generated marketing drafts.
- Explain features with concrete user value and avoid claims of universal calendar accuracy, worldwide coverage, guaranteed spiritual outcomes, or feature availability that has not been verified.
- Treat all currently available features as free; do not advertise legacy subscription tiers.
- Keep the public voice calm, clear, warm, and spiritual rather than heavy or preachy.

## What we explicitly rejected

- “Shoonaya is the void that holds all traditions.”
- Treating distinct Hindu/Sanatan, Sikh, Jain, and Buddhist teachings as interchangeable.
- Generic “ancient wisdom in your pocket” positioning that does not explain sacred time and everyday practice.
